## 2. What the primary record says an analysis must explain

1. IDDP-1 parted at 3 of about 160 connections in one quench. [K16]: "The
   predicted strain in the production casing that forms during quenching is
   however below the rupture strain of a new K55 material. Strength reduction
   due to factors such as corrosion, stress relaxation and creep, which all
   might have a great effect in this case are however difficult to estimate."
   A plasticity analysis without those terms does not reach rupture.
2. The wellhead tells how much of the string is free. [K16] Table 3:
   measured rise 368 mm in phase I and 440 mm in phase V (model 345 mm), and
   17 mm below the start after the quench: "suggesting large tensile forces
   due to thermal contraction of the casings".
3. Rate matters. [K16]: "for large axial strains to be less severe the
   quenching must be performed as slowly as possible." The quench was
   modelled there as a step ramped over 10 s.
4. Chemistry is a candidate beside the mechanics. [K16]:
   "Since the shut-in periods were lasting for several weeks up to 4 months,
   corrosion effects such as pitting and hydrogen embrittlement might have
   weakened the material."

## 3. Mechanisms the record names beyond these

- Thread shear at premium connections under a fast quench ([K17], quoted in
  3a).
- Hydrogen embrittlement and pitting from acid condensate during shut-ins
  ([K16], quoted above).
- High-velocity erosion in the annulus after the collapse ([K19]: the
  collapse "is considered to have lead to failures of two external casings").
